The WSJ article that changed EVERYTHING

On August 1, 1980, Maker's Mark became a real company.

At least, that's what company founder Bill Samuels Sr. said later on.

Because August 1st, 1980, was the day The Wall Street Journal ran a front-page feature on Maker's Mark. And nothing was the same for the company, or the Samuels family, ever again.

It told the story of the smallest distillery in America, doing everything backwards. Making whiskey slow. In small batches. And charging more for it, while the rest of the bourbon industry was battling to be cheapest.

Within days, letters and phone calls were pouring in from all over the country.

The distillery had to add more phone lines. And Bill Sr., along with his son, Bill Jr., answered letters every weekend.

For over a year.

Because everybody wanted to know:

Where can I buy this stuff??

And there was one big problem.

There wasn't enough to go around.

You see, Maker's Mark usually ages for six or seven years before it goes in a bottle.

That means every bottle available in 1980 went into a barrel back around 1974. When nobody saw this coming.

So even if every truck in America had been lined up at Maker's Mark's gate on August 2nd, 1980, there wasn't enough whiskey to ship.

But guess what?

The harder it was to find, the more people wanted it.

Suddenly, every bottle was a trophy.

Within a year, it was clear that if Bill Sr. wanted to get Maker's Mark into the hands of everyone writing letters, he was going to need some help.

Enter Hiram Walker & Sons, the spirits mega-company behind Canadian Club and Courvoisier.

Bill Sr. agreed to sell the brand to them in 1981 — and Maker's Mark got some serious help. Money for barrels. And warehouses. And access to a distribution network that would land their whiskey in stores from Portland, Maine to Portland, Oregon just as fast as the aging process allowed.

(Plus, a big, biiiiig check for the Samuels family. Not bad!)

And to their credit, the new owners wanted Bill Jr. to keep running things the same as Bill Sr. always had.

Same recipe.
Same patience.
Same red wax.

But this might be Maker's Mark's most lasting impact of all.

Remember how, before Maker's Mark, bourbon was considered the bottom of the bottom shelf?

Well, after 1980, they'd proved people would pay more for bourbon if it actually tasted good. And the rest of the bourbon industry noticed.

Fast.

  • 1984: Blanton's, the first bourbon sold as "single barrel"
  • 1988: Booker's, bottled straight from the barrel at full strength
  • 1992: Jim Beam's Small Batch Bourbon Collection: Booker's, Baker's, Basil Hayden's and Knob Creek
  • 1996: Woodford Reserve

Single barrel. Small batch. Barrel proof.

Fancy bottles.

Distillery tours.

So much of what we think of as "premium bourbon" today started on the trail blazed by Maker's Mark.

By a bottle dipped in red wax.
