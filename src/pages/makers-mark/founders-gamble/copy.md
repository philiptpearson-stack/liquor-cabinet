The Founder's Gamble.
Meet Bill Sr!
In 1953, a guy named Bill Samuels Sr. and his wife Margie bought a tiny, run-down distillery near Loretto, Kentucky.
Price tag? $35,000.
Now, Bill wasn't new to distilling. His family had been making whiskey a looong time. In fact, ancestor Robert Samuels was running a distilling operation all the way back in 1779!
But by the time Bill Sr. was running the show, the world had *changed*. First, Prohibition had shut down the T.W. Samuels Distillery. Then World War II shut it down again. And by 1943, Bill had had enough. He sold the whole thing. Family name and all.
But that was *far* from the end of the story. Within weeks, Bill was dreaming up his comeback!
Incorporating new companies.
Experimenting with new recipes.
And imagining the operation that would become Maker's Mark.
What is a "*Wheated*" bourbon??
Traditional bourbon is made of corn — plus a little rye for spice.

But Bill wanted something softer. Sweeter. More approachable for a post-Prohibition audience that prized "smoothness" above everything else. And he was **right** to worry about that. Bourbon had a *reputation* problem.
By the 1950s, bourbon was widely considered a working man's drink. The lowest tier. **The bottom shelf.** Nobody thought bourbon could be good, let alone smooth. And expensive? Get real.
That's why when Bill ditched the rye and used soft winter wheat as the "small grain," he was betting everything he had.
On himself.
His product.
And on the idea that bourbon's reputation could *change*.
It tastes expensive... and is.
So here comes Bill with his new-fangled whiskey and this crazy idea that bourbon could actually be ***good***.
And not "good for a bourbon," good.
***Good***, good. Period.
Good like the blended scotches that were all the rage in the big city markets were good. And *expensive* like them, too.
Five words. Maybe the cheekiest ad in bourbon history.
But think about it!
Every other bourbon was bragging about being cheap. Maker's Mark walked in and said, nah, man. Fuck that. We cost more. ***On purpose.***
And here's where the idea becomes genius. The magic didn't happen in the ad. It happened at the liquor store!
Let's say a prospective customer walks into the liquor store planning to grab his usual, ***smooth*** bottle of blended scotch. But, wait. Isn't that the Maker's Mark — the one from those crazy ads?
He checks the price. And yeah. It *is* expensive. For a bourbon.
But compared to the imported bottle of scotch he came in for? Right in the same ballpark. In fact, it's even a little cheaper!
So he buys it. Takes it home. Pours a glass. And, wait a minute! It's actually... *smooth!* Even a little sweet.
That's the genius of Maker's Mark. It's wheated, so it doesn't kick you in the pants like those el-cheap-o bourbons do.
Kentucky's best-kept secret
And so for the next 20-some years, that was the whole recipe.
Make it soft. Make it slow. Price it proud. Dip every bottle in red wax.
And Maker's grew. Slowly. Steadily. And almost entirely in Kentucky.
It grew by word of mouth.
It grew at dinner parties.
People who found it loved it. And they told their friends. And they told their families. Then, in 1980, *everything changed.*
Bill Sr.'s son, Bill Jr., had a buddy who now wrote for *The Wall Street Journal*. The writer came for a visit. And stayed three whole days.
And almost overnight, Maker's Mark became a national sensation.
