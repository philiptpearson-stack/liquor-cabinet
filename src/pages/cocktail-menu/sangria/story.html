Sangria Recipe

Sangria

Makes 1 drink

  • 4 oz Spanish red wine (like a Garnacha or Tempranillo)
  • 1 oz brandy
  • ½ oz orange liqueur
  • 1 oz orange juice (fresh squeezed)
  • ½ oz simple syrup
  • 1 oz soda water, to top (optional)

Glass: wine glass, over ice · Garnish: an orange wheel and fresh berries

For a pitcher (about 6): one bottle of red wine, ¾ cup brandy, ⅓ cup orange liqueur, ¾ cup orange juice and ⅓ cup simple syrup, plus a sliced orange, apple and lemon. Chill it for a few hours, or overnight. Add ice and soda just before serving.

Wine, fruit and a little brandy.

Made for a crowd.

The name comes from sangre, Spanish for “blood,” for its deep red color.

Spaniards were mixing wine and fruit for centuries before most Americans had heard of it. It caught on here after the Spanish Pavilion served it at the 1964 World's Fair in New York.

The secret is time. Make a pitcher a few hours ahead, and let the fruit soak into the wine.
