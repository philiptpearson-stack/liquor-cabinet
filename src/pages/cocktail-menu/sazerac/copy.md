The official cocktail of New Orleans.
*By a vote of 62 to 33.*
That was the Louisiana House of Representatives, in 2008. (It wasn't unanimous.)
Its roots go back to the 1830s, when a French Quarter apothecary named Antoine Peychaud mixed his family's bitters with cognac for his friends.
Today it's rye, a little sugar, Peychaud's bitters, and a glass rinsed with absinthe.
That rinse is the trick: pour in a splash of absinthe, swirl it to coat the glass, and pour out the rest. You'll smell it more than taste it.
Here's how you make one:
The Cocktail Menu · Classics
Sazerac
Makes − 1 + drink
2 oz rye whiskey
¼ oz simple syrup
3 dashes Peychaud's bitters
1 dash Angostura bitters
¼ oz absinthe, to rinse the glass
**Glass:** chilled rocks glass, no ice · **Garnish:** lemon peel
**Rinse the glass.** Pour a splash of absinthe into a chilled rocks glass, swirl it to coat, and pour out the rest.
**Combine ingredients.** Add the rye, simple syrup and both bitters to a mixing glass.
**Stir.** Fill with ice and stir until the drink is good and cold. Then, stir longer! (Seriously. You need some of that ice to melt. Water softens the alcohol-bite and brings the drink into **balance.** *Dilution is part of the recipe.*)
**Strain.** Strain into the rinsed glass, no ice.
**Garnish.** Twist a lemon peel over the top.
