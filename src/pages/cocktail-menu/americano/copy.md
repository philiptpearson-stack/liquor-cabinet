Before the Negroni,
there was the Americano.
Gaspare Campari served it at his café in Milan in the 1860s. He called it the Milano-Torino: Campari from Milan, sweet vermouth from Turin, and a splash of soda.
Nobody's sure where the name “Americano” came from. One story says American tourists loved it. Another says it comes from *amaro,* Italian for “bitter.”
Around 1919, a Count Negroni asked for his with gin instead of soda, and the Negroni was born.
Keep the soda, and you get the same bittersweet flavor at about half the strength. It's the perfect afternoon drink.
Here's how you make one:

Americano
Makes − 1 + drink
1 ½ oz Campari
1 ½ oz sweet vermouth
3 oz soda water

**Ice.** Fill a tall glass with ice.
**Campari and sweet vermouth.** Equal parts, right over the ice.
**Top with soda water.** Then one gentle stir.
**Garnish.** An orange wheel.
