Less sugar.
More rum.
In the early 1930s, Ernest Hemingway stopped into El Floridita in Havana, known as “the cradle of the Daiquiri.” He liked his first one, and asked for another with less sugar and more rum.
The owner, Constantino Ribalaigua, obliged, and added grapefruit juice and a few drops of maraschino liqueur. He called it the Papa Doble: “Papa” for Hemingway, and “Doble” for the double rum.
Hemingway later bragged about drinking 17 of them in one afternoon. (Please don't.)
Ours is a single. It's tart, bright and just a little sweet: the grapefruit and maraschino do the work the sugar used to.
Here's how you make one:

Hemingway Daiquiri
Makes − 1 + drink
2 oz white rum
¾ oz lime juice
½ oz grapefruit juice
¼ oz maraschino liqueur
**Glass:** coupe · **Garnish:** a lime wheel
**Combine ingredients.** Add the rum, lime juice, grapefruit juice and maraschino to your shaker.
**Shake.** Add ice and shake hard, until frosty cold.
**Strain.** Strain into a chilled coupe.
**Garnish.** A lime wheel.
Too tart? Add ¼ oz simple syrup. (Papa would never.)
