It's...um...an *acquired taste.*
But once you acquire it, there's no going back.
Bitter.
Sweet.
And beautifully **red.**
The Negroni is named after an actual Italian cowboy!
Really. It is.
Count Camillo Negroni was a Florentine aristocrat who had spent time in America as a cowboy, gambler, and fencing instructor.
And when you think about it? It *is* the kind of drink a drunken Italian cowboy would invent. The story goes that he asked his bartender for an Americano — but with gin instead of soda.
Here's how you make one:
The Cocktail Menu · Classics
Negroni
Makes − 1 + drink
2 oz gin
1 oz sweet vermouth
1 oz Campari
**Glass:** rocks, over one big ice cube · **Garnish:** orange peel
**Combine ingredients.** Add the gin, sweet vermouth and Campari to a mixing glass.
**Stir.** Fill with ice and stir until the drink is good and cold. Then, stir longer! (Seriously. You need some of that ice to melt. Water softens the alcohol-bite and brings the drink into **balance.** *Dilution is part of the recipe.*)
**Strain over a big cube.** Strain into a rocks glass over one large ice cube.
**Garnish.** Twist an orange peel over the drink, then drop it in.
