Rum Punch

Makes 1 drink

  • ½ oz lime juice (fresh squeezed)
  • 1 oz simple syrup
  • 1 ½ oz dark rum (Jamaican, if you can find it)
  • 2 oz cold water
  • 2 dashes Angostura bitters

Glass: rocks, over ice · Garnish: lime wheel, and a little grated nutmeg

For a crowd, measure in cups: 1 cup lime juice, 2 cups simple syrup, 3 cups rum, 4 cups cold water, and a good shake of bitters. Serve over ice.

One of sour, two of sweet,

three of strong and four of weak.

That old rhyme is the whole recipe. Nobody knows who wrote it, but Jamaica's been making punch by it for well over a century.

Sour is lime. Sweet is sugar syrup. Strong is rum. Weak is water (and the ice melting into it).

It's a formula, just like our Golden Ratio. And since it's parts, not ounces, it works for one glass or a whole bowl.

Punch itself is older still: in the Caribbean, people were ladling it out of big shared bowls by the 1690s.
