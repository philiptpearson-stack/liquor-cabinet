Nobody agrees who invented it.
Everybody agrees it fixes brunch.
Bartender Fernand Petiot said he did, at Harry's New York Bar in Paris in the 1920s. Comedian George Jessel said he did, in Palm Beach in 1927.
Petiot certainly made it famous. In 1934 he brought it to the King Cole Bar at New York's St. Regis, where for a while it was called the Red Snapper. “Bloody Mary” seemed too rude.
The name first showed up in print in 1939.
It's the one cocktail you season like soup: taste it, then add more spice, salt or lemon until it's right.
Here's how you make one:

**Glass:** highball, over ice · **Garnish:** celery stalk and a lemon wedge
**Ice the glass.** Fill a tall glass with ice.
**Vodka, tomato and lemon.** Pour them in.
**Season it.** Worcestershire, hot sauce, a pinch of salt and a grind of pepper.
**Stir, and taste.** Then adjust: more spice, salt or lemon.
**Garnish.** A celery stalk and a lemon wedge.
