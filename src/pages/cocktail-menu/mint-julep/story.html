Mint Julep

Makes 1 drink

  • 2 ½ oz bourbon
  • ½ oz simple syrup
  • 8 mint leaves

Glass: julep cup (or a rocks glass), packed with crushed ice · Garnish: a big bouquet of mint

The Kentucky Derby's drink since the 1930s.

But the julep is far older than the Derby. An English traveler in America around 1800 described it as “a dram of spirituous liquor that has mint steeped in it.”

The idea goes back further still, to Persia, where people mixed syrup with mint or rose water.

The trick is the ice. Pack the cup with crushed ice and stir until the metal frosts over.

Then hold it by the rim or the base, so your warm hand doesn't melt the frost.
