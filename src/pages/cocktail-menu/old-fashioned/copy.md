**It's what the word cocktail *used* to mean!**
In 1806, a New York newspaper defined a “cock-tail” like this:
“A stimulating liquor, composed of spirits of any kind, sugar, water, and bitters.”
— The Balance, and Columbian Repository, Hudson, New York, 1806
And even by then, the formula was old.
Like, OLD-old.
People were sweetening their (harsh!) spirits with sugar and balancing them with bitters and water by the early 1700s!
But remember! In those days, the word *cocktail* meant that specific formula, and not anything else.
If you wanted a cocktail, you asked for a cocktail.
But if you wanted a julep, you asked for that instead.
And...if you wanted a smash, a flip, a fizz, or a sling, well. You get it.
So when the 1880s rolled around and bartenders started experimenting with all sorts of new ingredients in their cocktails — curaçao, maraschino, or even *vermouth* (Manhattan! Martini!) — people who wanted it the way they'd always had it found a new way to order:
*“Make my cocktail the Old Fashioned way.”*
And it stuck.
Here's how you make one:
The Cocktail Menu · Classics
Old Fashioned
Makes − 1 + drink
2 oz bourbon or rye
¼ oz–½ oz demerara simple, to taste
4 dashes Angostura bitters
2 dashes orange bitters
**Glass:** rocks, over one big ice cube · **Garnish:** orange peel
**Combine ingredients.** Add the whiskey, demerara simple and both bitters to a mixing glass.
**Stir.** Fill with ice and stir until the drink is good and cold. Then, stir longer! (Seriously. You need some of that ice to melt. Water softens the alcohol-bite and brings the drink into **balance.** *Dilution is part of the recipe.*)
**Strain over a big cube.** Strain into a rocks glass over one large ice cube.
**Garnish.** Twist an orange peel over the drink, then drop it in.
