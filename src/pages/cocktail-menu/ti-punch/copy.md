*That's the tradition.*
“Ti’” is Creole for “petit.” So: a little punch, the everyday drink of Martinique and Guadeloupe, in the French Caribbean.
It's just three things: rhum agricole (rum made from fresh sugarcane juice), a coin of lime and a little cane syrup. Traditionally, no ice. There was no ice in the cane fields.
In Martinique, the bottles go on the table and everybody makes their own, as strong or as sweet as they like. As the saying goes: *chacun prépare sa propre mort.* Everyone prepares their own death.
Here's how you make one:

Ti’ Punch
Makes − 1 + drink
2 oz rhum agricole blanc
1 barspoon cane syrup
1 coin of lime, with a little flesh on it
**Glass:** small rocks glass, no ice · **Garnish:** a coin of lime, in the drink
**Cut a lime coin.** Slice a coin from the side of a lime, about the size of a quarter, with a little flesh on it. Squeeze it into a small glass, and drop it in.
**Add the cane syrup.** A bar spoon, or to taste.
**Add the rhum.**
**Swizzle.** Stir it together until the syrup dissolves. Add an ice cube if you like. (We won't tell.)
