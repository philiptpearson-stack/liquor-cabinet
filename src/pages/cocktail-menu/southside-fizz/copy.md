Chicago's Prohibition Mojito?
Maybe!!
Legend ties this classic to Chicago's South Side (and to Al Capone!) during the 1920s.
But legends are just that. Another ties it to the South Side Sportsmen's Club on Long Island, around the same time.
It's basically a Tom Collins, with mint. It's also a Mojito, with gin.
Because just like every drink using the Golden Ratio, it's a sour — with tiny little goofy variations.
Here's how you make one:
The Cocktail Menu · Classics
Southside Fizz
Makes − 1 + drink
2 oz gin
1 oz simple syrup
1 oz lime juice
6 mint leaves
2 oz soda water
**Glass:** highball, over ice
**Garnish:** mint sprig
**Combine ingredients.** Add the gin, simple syrup, lime juice and mint leaves to your shaker.
**Shake.** Add ice and shake hard. It wakes up the mint.
**Strain over ice.** Strain into a highball glass full of fresh ice.
**Top with soda water.**
**Garnish.** A sprig of mint.
