*Margarita* means “daisy” in Spanish.
That's not a coincidence.
In the late 1800s, a Daisy was a whole family of drinks: a spirit, fresh citrus and something sweet, often orange liqueur.
By 1936, newspapers were writing about the Tequila Daisy at bars in Tijuana.
Say that in Spanish, and you get a Margarita. The name first showed up in print in 1953, and two years later a tequila importer was pushing it all across the country.
It's also the Golden Ratio, plain as day: two parts tequila, one part orange liqueur, one part lime.
The salt rim earns its place. A little salt makes the lime taste brighter and tames the bite of the tequila.
Here's how you make a *great* Margarita:
The Cocktail Menu · Classics
Margarita
Makes − 1 + drink
2 oz tequila (blanco)
1 oz orange liqueur
1 oz lime juice (fresh squeezed)
**Glass:** coupe, with a salted rim (or on the rocks) · **Garnish:** lime wheel
Salt the rim with coarse salt. Salt just half of it, if you like, so every sip can be your choice.
**Salt the rim.** Rub a lime wedge around the outside of the rim of a chilled coupe, then roll it in coarse salt.
**Combine ingredients.** Add the tequila, orange liqueur and lime juice to your shaker.
**Shake.** Add ice and shake hard, until frosty cold.
**Strain.** Strain into the salted coupe.
**Garnish.** A lime wheel on the rim.
