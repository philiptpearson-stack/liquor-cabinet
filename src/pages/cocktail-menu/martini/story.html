The Cocktail Menu: Martini.

It started out as a Manhattan.

No, really. Just with gin.

In the 1880s, bartenders were swapping the whiskey in a Manhattan for gin. The first printed recipe for the Martini's ancestor, the Martinez, barely bothered with details:

That early version was sweet: Old Tom gin, sweet vermouth, a little maraschino or curaçao, and bitters.

But tastes were changing, and bartenders started drying it out.

Dry French vermouth in place of sweet.

Crisp London dry gin in place of Old Tom.

No more sugar at all.

By 1904, recipes for a “Dry Martini” were showing up in bar books. And it just kept getting drier: by 1950, Ernest Hemingway was writing about Martinis mixed fifteen parts gin to one part vermouth.

Then, in 1958, James Bond ordered his “shaken and not stirred.”

Here's the thing, though: a Martini is all booze. No citrus. So by our rule of thumb, you stir it.

Shaking makes it cloudy, full of little chips of ice. Stirring keeps it crystal clear and silky.

Sorry, Mr. Bond.

Here's how you make a proper Martini:

The Cocktail Menu · Classics

Martini

Makes 1 drink

  • 2 ½ oz gin
  • ½ oz dry vermouth
  • 1 dash orange bitters (optional)

Glass: martini glass · Garnish: olives or a lemon twist

Like it drier? Use less vermouth. Like it dirty? Add a splash of olive brine.
