Lost for most of a century.
Found in Seattle.
The Detroit Athletic Club had it on the menu in 1916, at twice the price of a Manhattan. Then Prohibition came, and it all but disappeared.
In 2003, Seattle bartender Murray Stenson found the recipe in an old cocktail book and put it on the menu at the Zig Zag Café. Other bartenders tasted it, and it took off.
The formula is as simple as it gets: equal parts gin, green Chartreuse, maraschino liqueur and lime. Herbal, sweet, sour and sharp, all at once.
That equal-parts formula became a template for new drinks. (The Paper Plane uses it, too.)
Here's how you make one:

Last Word
Makes − 1 + drink
¾ oz gin
¾ oz green Chartreuse
¾ oz maraschino liqueur
¾ oz lime juice (fresh squeezed)
**Glass:** coupe · **Garnish:** a cherry
**Combine ingredients.** Equal parts gin, green Chartreuse, maraschino liqueur and lime juice, into your shaker.
**Shake.** Add ice and shake hard, until frosty cold.
**Strain.** Strain into a chilled coupe.
**Garnish.** A cherry.
