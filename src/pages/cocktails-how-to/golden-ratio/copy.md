Cocktails How-To: The Golden ***Ratio.***
The Short Version
2 : 1 : 1 · spirit : sweet : sour
The formula
Two parts spirit, one part sweetener, one part sour/bitter. It's the formula behind most of the classics.
The goal
To achieve perfect balance. The sweetener balances the harshness of the spirit; while the sour balances out the sweet.
Here's a little bartender secret:
Cocktails are not just recipes.
They're ***formulas*.**
Make the formula flawlessly, and you can make every cocktail on the menu.

See the pattern?
If you can make an excellent Margarita, you can make an excellent Whiskey Sour. Or a Lemon Drop.
If you can make an excellent Negroni, you can make a great Boulevardier too. (Plus, with a slight variation for concentrated bitters, an amazing Old Fashioned, Manhattan or Martini!)
Because it's all about balance
The sweetener balances the spirit — it smoothes out the harsh edges.
And the acid/bitters balances the sweetness, so the drink doesn't taste like candy.
One formula, whole families
Once you know the formula, you'll start seeing it everywhere.
A Collins? That's just a sour with bubbles. Gin, lemon and simple syrup, topped with soda water.
A Vieux Carré? It's just an Old Fashioned with (*very*) different seasonings.
One CRUCIAL Distinction: Bitters that pour vs. bitters that dash
Campari is made to be sipped, so you can pour it by the ounce and it slots right into the formula.
Concentrated bitters, like Angostura, are different. You don't pour them. You dash them.
So how many dashes does it take to balance a drink the way acid would?
More than you think.
In an Old Fashioned, start with three to four good dashes, stir well, taste, and add more. Don't be shy! Bitter-bitter, stir-stir-stir makes a great drink!
