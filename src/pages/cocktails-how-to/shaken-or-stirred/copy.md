Cocktails How-To: Shaken or ***Stirred?***
The Short Version
Stir Bitters. Shake Citrus.
Dilute well!
Here's the rule of thumb:
Cocktails with bitters, you stir. Drinks with citrus (like lemon or lime juice), you shake.
And the reason why is surprisingly straightforward.
**Shaking** gives you dilution + aeration. It's how you get balance AND the lovely, frothy texture of a Margarita or a Lemon Drop.
**Stirring** gives you dilution + control. And for drinks with concentrated bitters, like an Old Fashioned or a Manhattan, control over *intensity of flavor* is the whole ballgame.
Because it's all about *dilution*!
This is the single ingredient that can take a *meh* mixed drink to a *great* at-home cocktail:
Water!
Really.
100% serious.
Dilution is what turns a collection of ingredients into a finished cocktail. And it's a step overlooked by almost everyone.
Shaking, or stirring, is not **only** about making your drink cold. It's about adding water.
That water softens the alcohol's bite and adjusts the intensity of everything else in the glass: the sweetness, the bitterness, the citrus.
Too little, and the drink can taste harsh or overpowering. Too much, and you lose the flavors you wanted in the first place.
*Dilution is part of the recipe!*
So, how much dilution is right?
About 25% is a useful starting point.
That might sound like a lot, but it's true.
Roughly a quarter of your finished volume can come from melted ice. For a shaken drink, shake vigorously for around 10 seconds.
*And we do mean vigorously!*
For a stirred drink, it usually means stirring longer than you think.
Fill your mixing glass with ice and stir until the drink is good and cold. Then, stir longer!
*And taste it along the way.*
Because shaken or stirred, dilution is how all those strong individual ingredients start tasting like one great drink.
