The Liquor Cabinet01 / Fermentation

Fermentation.

You’ve heard of fermentation, right? The process by which beer, wine and all of their little cousins are born? It’s sort of the birds and bees of alcohol!

Basically, a super-tiny microorganism called yeast turns sugar from plants into alcohol.

Yeast has one job in life. It eats sugar. And then it secretes alcohol. (Plus a little CO₂. That’s the bubbles.)

Thanks, Yeast!

Our yeast-buds are everywhere. Hanging around on grape skins and tree leaves. Even floating in the air. Just waiting to find some sugar to turn into yummy booze.

But wait. Beer and wine? When do humans get involved??

Well, let’s back up…

Like, waaaay up.

Turns out, our yeast-buds were making booze way before humans were doing human-stuff.

Imagine an ancient bear goes sauntering past some grapevines. He kind of blonks into the grapes and smooshes them.

Now those sugary grape-insides are wide open. And guess who’s been hanging around on the vines? Our little buddies, the yeast!

A couple of days go by and our yeast-buds get to work.Consuming sugars…Releasing CO₂…Secreting alcohol…

And would you look at that? Fermentation happens! All on its own.

Thanks, Bear!!
Thanks, Yeast!!

And this is where humans come in.

Picture a quiet day in the prehistoric forest. Along comes one of our extremely ancient ancestors, following that rich, sweet smell.

He finds the fermented fruit.He eats the fermented fruit.And bazonga!

Primitive Drunkenness!!

So here’s everything you need to know about fermentation:

Yeast + Sugar = CO₂ + Alcohol

And Alcohol + People eventually = beer, wine, pulque and sake. Then, thousands of years later, every spirit in the cabinet. Whiskey, brandy, rum, even tequila!

So fermented alcohol isn’t some weird side note in history.

It’s human history. Our history.

In fact, some archaeologists even think it’s what helped us go from wandering little weirdos to settled farmers!

Long before spirits.

And long before bartenders.

And long, long before cocktails and wine lists.

There was yeast, sugar, and the beautiful human realization that spoiled fruit might actually be FUN.
