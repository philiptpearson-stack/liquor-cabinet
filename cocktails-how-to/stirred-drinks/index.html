Cocktails How-To: Stirred Drinks.

The Short Version
spirit · sugar · water · bitters
Stir it cold. Then, stir longer.
The formula
Spirit, a little sugar and a few good dashes of bitters. The ice adds the water.
The goal
Cold, silky and in balance. There's no juice, so you stir it instead of shaking it.

Spirit, sugar, water and bitters.

That's the original Cocktail. It's also the very first definition of the word ever printed:

Every stirred drink on our menu grows from that one formula.

How the Old Fashioned got its name

By the 1870s, bartenders were “improving” the whiskey Cocktail with a splash of absinthe, curaçao or maraschino.

Then came vermouth, and with it the Manhattan.

Plenty of drinkers just wanted the plain one. So they asked for an old-fashioned whiskey Cocktail. The name stuck.

Why stir?

Stirred drinks are all booze: spirits, vermouth, bitters. No juice, no egg, no cream.

Shaking whips in air and tiny chips of ice, which is just what a sour wants. Stirring chills the drink without the froth, so it stays clear and silky.

Stir longer than you think.

Fill with ice and stir until the drink is good and cold. Then, stir longer!

(Seriously. You need some of that ice to melt. Water softens the alcohol-bite and brings the drink into balance. Dilution is part of the recipe.)

That's the water in that very first definition.

Now, the bitters.

Concentrated bitters, like Angostura, are different. A dash is only a few drops, but those drops are what balance the sugar.

So how many dashes does it take to bring an Old Fashioned into balance?

More than you think.

In an Old Fashioned, start with three to four good dashes, stir well, taste, and add more if you like.

Don't be shy!

Bitter-bitter, stir-stir-stir makes a great drink!
